# SOP-SAF-02 — Pedestrian–MHE Segregation, Hazard Reporting & Emergency Response

**Site:** All warehouse operations | **Applies to:** All warehouse operatives, including pedestrian-only roles
**Regulatory basis:** RD 486/1997 (workplace safety conditions); Ley 31/1995 Art. 19 (training and information duty)
**Version:** 1.4 | **Effective:** 2025-11-01 | **Review cycle:** Annual

## 1. Pedestrian–MHE segregation and right of way

The warehouse floor is marked into pedestrian walkways (green floor markings) and MHE operating lanes (yellow floor markings with hazard chevrons).

1. Pedestrians must use marked walkways at all times. Crossing an MHE lane is only permitted at a marked crossing point, after making eye contact with any approaching MHE operator.
2. MHE (powered pallet trucks, reach trucks) always has right of way within its operating lane. Pedestrians do not have right of way in a yellow-marked lane even at a crossing point — they must wait for a clear signal from the operator.
3. MHE operators must sound the horn at every blind corner and every marked crossing point, whether or not a pedestrian is visible.
4. Running is prohibited anywhere on the warehouse floor, in both pedestrian and MHE zones.

A common misconception is that a pedestrian at a marked crossing point has priority, similar to a road crosswalk. Under this SOP, the MHE lane retains right of way at all times — the marked crossing only designates *where* a pedestrian may attempt to cross, never that they have priority to do so.

## 2. Hazard and near-miss reporting

1. Any hazard (spill, damaged racking, blocked emergency exit, PPE non-compliance) or near-miss (a pedestrian/MHE conflict, a near-fall, equipment malfunction without injury) must be reported before the end of the shift in which it occurred, using the site incident terminal or the paper near-miss card if the terminal is unavailable.
2. Reporting a near-miss is not an admission of fault and carries no disciplinary consequence — the purpose is to catch a pattern before it causes an injury.
3. Reports must include: location, time, what happened, and what stopped it becoming an injury.

## 3. Emergency evacuation

1. On alarm, stop all MHE immediately, lower forks/loads to the floor, and switch off the ignition/power before dismounting.
2. Proceed via the nearest marked exit to your zone's designated muster point (muster point locations are posted at every zone entrance).
3. Do not re-enter the building for any reason, including retrieving personal items, until a supervisor confirms all-clear.
4. Report to your shift supervisor at the muster point for roll call. Do not leave the muster point until roll call is confirmed complete.

## 4. Spill response

1. Non-hazardous spills (water, non-chemical liquids): cordon the area with a warning cone, absorb with the floor-mounted spill kit, dispose of absorbent material in general waste, remove the cone once the floor is dry.
2. Hazardous spills (any chemical, fuel, or unidentified substance): do not attempt to clean up. Cordon the area at a minimum 3-metre radius, evacuate the immediate zone, and notify the shift supervisor and site safety officer immediately. Only personnel with hazardous-spill training may handle cleanup.
3. If in doubt whether a spill is hazardous, always treat it as hazardous under §4.2.
