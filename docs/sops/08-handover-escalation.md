# SOP-BEH-01 — Shift Handover & Escalation Thresholds

**Site:** All warehouse operations | **Applies to:** All warehouse operatives
**Regulatory basis:** Ley 31/1995 Art. 19 (worker information and training duty) — communication of hazards and open exceptions across shifts is treated as an information-duty matter, not only an operational courtesy
**Version:** 1.2 | **Effective:** 2025-08-15 | **Review cycle:** Annual

## 1. Shift handover: what must be communicated and to whom

1. Handover occurs directly between the outgoing and incoming operative on the same task/zone — not via a third party or a general note left unread.
2. Mandatory handover content: any open exception (short pick, damage code, blocked stock — see SOP-SYS-01 §4) in your zone, any equipment tagged out of service (see SOP-EQP-01 §1), any unresolved near-miss or hazard from your shift (see SOP-SAF-02 §2), and the status of any in-progress task that will not be complete at shift end.
3. Handover must be verbal and confirmed understood by the incoming operative — reading a status board alone does not satisfy this requirement, since a board can be misread or out of date.
4. The shift supervisor must be copied on any handover item involving an open safety exception (near-miss, hazard, equipment fault) — this is in addition to, not instead of, the operative-to-operative handover.

## 2. Escalation thresholds: what to stop for vs. report after

Some conditions require stopping work immediately and escalating in real time; others are logged and reported through normal channels without stopping the task. Confusing the two is itself a safety risk — over-escalating erodes trust in the escalation channel, under-escalating delays response to a genuine risk.

**Stop and escalate immediately** (do not continue the task):
- Any injury, regardless of apparent severity.
- Any hazardous spill (see SOP-SAF-02 §4.2).
- A safety device failure discovered mid-task (e.g. PPT emergency reverse fails during operation, not just at pre-use check).
- A cold chain breach beyond exposure limit (see SOP-PRC-02 §4.2–4.3).
- Any sign of harassment, threat, or distress involving yourself or a colleague.

**Report through normal channels** (log and continue, unless the item also meets a stop condition above):
- A single short pick or quantity variance within normal tolerance.
- A non-hazardous spill, once contained per SOP-SAF-02 §4.1.
- Routine equipment tag-out for a known, non-safety-critical fault (e.g. cosmetic damage not affecting operation).
- A recurring but individually minor variance pattern (see SOP-PRC-03 §1.4) — flagged for supervisor review, not an immediate stop.

If a condition is not clearly on either list, the default is to escalate — this SOP treats an unnecessary escalation as a lower cost than a missed one.
