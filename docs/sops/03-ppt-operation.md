# SOP-EQP-01 — Powered Pallet Truck: Pre-Use Inspection & Operation

**Site:** All warehouse operations | **Applies to:** Operatives certified for powered pallet truck (PPT) use
**Regulatory basis:** RD 1215/1997 (minimum safety requirements for use of work equipment); practical competence is additionally assessed by a certified human trainer before independent use is authorised
**Version:** 3.0 | **Effective:** 2026-02-01 | **Review cycle:** Annual

## 1. Pre-use inspection checklist

Before every shift, and before any operative other than the previous operator resumes use of a PPT, complete the following checklist. Do not operate a PPT that fails any item — tag it out and report to the shift supervisor.

1. Visual check: forks for cracks or bends, wheels/casters for wear or embedded debris, no visible hydraulic fluid leaks.
2. Battery charge indicator reads above the minimum operating threshold marked on the dash.
3. Horn, brakes, and emergency reverse (belly button / presence sensor) all function correctly — test each before moving.
4. Load backrest and forks are securely mounted, no play or looseness.
5. Emergency stop button is unobstructed and functions when pressed.

A PPT that passes inspection is logged as "in service" on the equipment board with operator initials and time. A PPT that fails is logged as "out of service" with the specific failed item noted — never leave a failed check unlogged.

## 2. Operation: load stability, gradients, cornering

1. Load stability: forks must be fully inserted under the pallet before lifting; never lift a load that is not square on the forks. Load must not obstruct forward visibility — if it does, drive in reverse with a spotter or use a mirror-equipped route.
2. Gradients: PPTs are only rated for the site's loading dock ramps (max 6% grade) at walking pace. On any gradient, keep the load on the uphill side of the truck (i.e., drive forwards going up, reverse going down) to prevent the load sliding off the forks.
3. Cornering: reduce speed before entering a turn, not during it. Never turn at a marked pedestrian crossing (see SOP-SAF-02 §1). Sound the horn before every blind corner.
4. Maximum travel speed is walking pace throughout the warehouse floor — a PPT is a pedestrian-speed device, not a substitute for a reach truck on longer runs.

## 3. Common errors

The most frequent inspection failure that operatives skip is the emergency reverse / presence sensor test in §1.3 — it is easy to assume it works because the truck moved normally in the previous shift. It must be tested at the start of every shift regardless of who used it last.
